# Chapter I

## You Are Dead. And You Were an Absolute Bastard.

You are dead.

This is particularly inconvenient because you have spent your entire life being an absolute bastard.

You've been cruel, greedy, exploitative and generally quite dedicated to making other sentient beings miserable.

Whenever compassion arose, you quickly suppressed it with the reassuring thought that everyone was probably exaggerating.

You once considered becoming vegetarian, then decided that your personal dietary convenience outweighed the suffering of several thousand animals.

You've lied to people who trusted you. You've been delighted by your enemies' misfortunes. You've cultivated resentment like a prize-winning orchid.

And now you've had a heart attack.

Your body is on the floor.

Welcome to the first extraordinary revelation of Tibetan Buddhism.

**Your heart stopping is not necessarily the same event as the completion of the subtle death process described in its tantric physiology.**

Modern medicine and Tibetan tantra use different criteria. Biomedicine investigates circulation, brain function and irreversible biological failure. Tibetan death teachings describe a further progressive dissolution of the supports for ordinary consciousness.

Do not confuse the latter with an experimentally verified physiological sequence.

### The demolition of your universe

In a widespread Tibetan tantric account, death unfolds through eight progressively subtler stages.

First comes the dissolution of the four elements.

These are not four chemical substances physically disappearing. They are traditional categories of bodily function and experience.

**Earth dissolves into water.**

Your body loses its strength. Its solidity, its reassuring heaviness, its ability to hold itself upright begins failing.

An internal appearance resembling a mirage may arise.

You think, *I seem rather unwell.*

Mate, you're dying.

**Water dissolves into fire.**

The moisture and fluid functions associated with this elemental system weaken. The mouth may become dry. Sensory capacities deteriorate.

An appearance compared with smoke may arise.

You think, *Perhaps I should call somebody.*

You should have done that about ten minutes ago.

**Fire dissolves into wind.**

The body loses warmth, and the capacity to regulate its ordinary biological activity deteriorates.

The traditional internal appearance resembles sparks or fireflies.

Your familiar sensory world is breaking down.

**Wind dissolves into consciousness.**

Breathing ceases.

The coarser forms of mental activity begin to dissolve, leaving subtler processes described through tantric notions of energy-winds and consciousness.

The inner appearance is likened to a flickering flame.

And now the particularly interesting part begins.

The Tibetan system describes four further stages\:

White appearance. Red increase. Black near-attainment. Clear light.

These are not claims that the brain literally produces four coloured screens. They are descriptions of increasingly subtle conscious experience within a tantric model of mind and body. \[[The death process](https://thubtenchodron.org/2020/06/eight-stages-dying/?utm_source=chatgpt.com); [The Guhyasamaja Presentation of the Process of Death](https://studybuddhism.com/en/advanced-studies/vajrayana/tantra-advanced/the-process-of-death-bardo-and-rebirth/the-guhyasamaja-presentation-of-the-process-of-death?utm_source=chatgpt.com)\]

### The final four stages

Imagine that every thought you have ever had depends upon an immense architecture of perception, memory, bodily sensation and conceptual activity.

Now begin dismantling that architecture.

Not by removing one particular thought, but by taking away progressively more fundamental supports for ordinary thinking.

First, a vivid white appearance.

Then a red appearance.

Then darkness.

Finally, according to the tradition, an extraordinary luminosity emerges when the grosser processes have subsided.

This is the **clear light of death**\.

And here the Tibetan tradition makes its most outrageous claim.

The dissolution of ordinary experience can expose the most fundamental nature of mind.

You do not have to manufacture it.

You have to recognize it.

Which, given your lifetime of treating every passing thought as a personal emergency, is going to be quite challenging.

### The greatest opportunity you have ever immediately wasted

All the usual things you identified with are gone.

Your body. Your name. Your possessions. Your impressive collection of grievances.

For an instant, in the Tibetan account, the ordinary apparatus of self-reference no longer functions as it did.

Here is the opportunity.

If the nature of this luminous awareness is recognized directly, the cycle of compulsory identification can be interrupted.

Notice the important distinction.

Liberation is not the ordinary ego discovering that it is secretly indestructible.

It is the undoing of the ignorance through which an intrinsically existent ego was imagined in the first place.

That is a very different proposition.

Unfortunately, you have not practised.

Your last genuine moment of self-examination was approximately fourteen years ago, when you wondered whether other people found you unpleasant.

You concluded that they were jealous.

And so the clear light dawns.

The opportunity of liberation appears.

And your habitual ignorance remains.

**You miss it.**

Astonishing.

The cosmos has offered you the possibility of release from beginningless suffering, and you've reacted with the spiritual equivalent of clicking *Remind Me Tomorrow*\.

### Now where the fuck are you?

This is where we must abandon a common Western assumption.

Tibetan Buddhism does not generally posit a permanent soul that exits the body and travels to a different address.

It describes a causally continuous stream of consciousness without an independently existing self.

That is how rebirth is meant to work.

Consider a flame lighting another flame.

The second flame is not numerically identical to the first.

But neither has it arisen independently of the first.

There is causal continuity without a permanent object migrating between locations.

The Buddhist claim is that a mental continuum can continue through death and rebirth in an analogous way.

Whether such continuity actually survives irreversible death remains scientifically unestablished. But philosophically, the model is more sophisticated than a ghost wearing a slightly different body each Tuesday. \[[Buddha (Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy/Fall 2024 Edition)](https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/fall2024/entries/buddha/?utm_source=chatgpt.com)\]

And in the Tibetan account, your continuum has not achieved liberation.

Its habituated tendencies are still operative.

Which brings us to karma.

### Karma is not divine customer service

You are expecting a judge.

Some enormous cosmic magistrate with an impossibly long beard and a detailed inventory of your crimes.

You would particularly like to dispute several of those crimes.

Perhaps negotiate a reduction.

Maybe point out that you've occasionally donated to charity.

Unfortunately, Buddhism doesn't give you a creator God administering the cosmic justice system.

**Karma is intentional action and its causal consequences.**

In Buddhist theory, your actions of body, speech and mind leave causal potentials that can ripen into later experiences.

And these are not merely changes in psychological habit, although that is part of the picture.

Traditional Buddhism asserts that karmic potentials also condition future rebirths and experiences across lifetimes.

There is no independent judge deciding what you deserve.

Nor is karma simply the claim that bad people are naturally unhappy.

Some appalling people live very comfortable lives. Buddhism knows this.

The claim is that karmic effects may mature under conditions far removed from the actions that produced them.

It is a moral-causal account of saṃsāra, not a promise of immediate fairness.

And importantly, everything unpleasant that happens to someone is not thereby proof that they deserved it.

Now, your problem is that you've spent decades intentionally cultivating cruelty.

Hatred has become familiar.

Greed has become familiar.

Fear has become familiar.

And when your ordinary sensory environment falls away, the habitual mental tendencies remain central to how experience unfolds.

Here is the explanatory power of the Tibetan model.

**What you repeatedly generate in consciousness conditions what you are disposed to experience.**

Imagine a dream.

When you become frightened, the dream sometimes grows increasingly threatening.

You run.

You expect pursuit.

Something pursues you.

Your fear reinforces the reality of the threat.

You have entered a self-amplifying loop.

That doesn't prove rebirth. It does give us an intelligible analogy for the Tibetan explanation of how karmically conditioned experience can become a seemingly objective world.

And, my unfortunate friend, you are about to discover just how thoroughly you've trained yourself in fear.

### Congratulations. You've discovered hell.

The Tibetan tradition recognizes six broad realms of saṃsāric rebirth: gods, demi-gods, humans, animals, hungry ghosts and hell beings.

These are not simply six moods of the human psyche, though they can also be interpreted psychologically.

Within traditional Buddhist cosmology, they are genuine modes of embodied existence conditioned by karma.

Hell is not eternal.

Nor is it administered as punishment by a supreme creator.

It is a particular suffering-filled condition of existence, arising through causes and eventually ceasing when the relevant supporting conditions are exhausted.

Now imagine the karmic continuum that has spent an entire human lifetime nurturing malice entering an existence overwhelmingly structured by aversion and terror.

Your ordinary human world has vanished.

And suddenly, everything burns.

You run.

The ground burns.

You look for escape.

The horizon burns.

You hate the experience.

The hatred is overwhelming.

The terrifying irony is that the very mental quality which helped condition the experience is now repeatedly reactivated by it.

You are trapped in a world where your own reactive tendencies have become almost impossible to escape.

The hot and cold hells of Buddhist cosmology make the suffering vividly concrete.

Not because a divine sadist decided to open a particularly unpleasant theme park.

But because, according to the karmic doctrine, profoundly destructive causes can produce correspondingly terrible results.

The point is not that every malicious thought automatically generates a specific infernal destination. Actual karmic ripening is held to be highly complex, involving innumerable past actions and conditions.

And here's the really important part.

**Even this is impermanent.**

Eventually, the conditions sustaining this particular existence cease.

Which means you die again.

There is no cosmic achievement for surviving your first death only to make it into hell.

No award.

No congratulatory email.

Just another transition.

And somehow, after vast and potentially inconceivable suffering, other karmic potentials mature.

A different rebirth becomes possible.

### You are alive\!

You're a human being again.

You have little hands.

Tiny feet.

A remarkably loud voice.

Someone picks you up and says that you are beautiful.

You are, in fact, covered in bodily fluids and currently capable of expressing only the most rudimentary demands.

Nevertheless, you've made it.

A human birth.

According to Tibetan Buddhism, this is an extraordinary opportunity.

Not because humans are metaphysically superior in every respect.

But because a human life can contain a particularly useful mixture of suffering, intelligence, leisure, ethical choice and access to spiritual teachings.

Too much suffering, and practice becomes almost impossible.

Too much pleasure, and there may be no urgency to practise.

Human existence can occupy a productive middle ground.

And you've just escaped an appalling realm of suffering.

One might hope you have learned something.

Unfortunately, your new brain has no accessible autobiographical memory of that infernal experience.

You grow up.

Acquire a personality.

Start forming opinions.

Become mildly obsessed with what other people think of you.

And one afternoon, at the age of seventeen, someone insults you.

A familiar anger rises.

Oh, for fuck's sake.

**It's happening again.**

You have acquired another body, another name, another world and another opportunity.

But the fundamental ignorance remains unexamined.

And that, according to the Tibetan account, is the tragedy of saṃsāra.

Not simply that you die.

But that you keep mistaking the next arising identity for the whole truth of what you are.

And because that mistake remains uncorrected, the machinery keeps turning.

## The deeper question

Why is the Tibetan tradition so concerned with what happens at death?

Because death is understood as an extraordinary opportunity to reveal the distinction between awareness and the appearances ordinarily mistaken for a permanent self.

But the teachings are equally concerned with **what happens while you are alive**\.

Every night you dream, constructing an entire world that feels convincing until you awaken.

Every day emotions arise, become apparently solid, and dissolve.

Every moment some experience appears and is appropriated as *mine*\.

The death process is not treated as an entirely separate metaphysical curiosity.

It is the most radical instance of a process already occurring throughout life.

This is why the tradition teaches six bardos, not merely an interval between two physical incarnations. The natural bardo of life, the bardos of meditation and dreaming, the painful bardo of dying, the luminous bardo of dharmatā, and the karmic bardo of becoming are interconnected opportunities for recognition. \[[Six bardos](https://rigpawiki.org/index.php?title=Six_bardos&utm_source=chatgpt.com)\]

We have begun with the worst plausible trajectory: destructive karmic conditioning, failure to recognize the clear light, an unhappy rebirth, and eventually another opportunity as a human.

The next death is going to be considerably stranger.

Because this time you're going to encounter the peaceful and wrathful deities.

And the terrifying thing isn't what they do to you.

It's that, according to the text, **they are manifestations of the very nature of mind you've spent your entire life failing to recognize**\. \[[Peaceful and Wrathful Deities of the Bardo](https://rubinmuseum.org/collection/c2006-66-17/?utm_source=chatgpt.com); [University of Virginia Library Online Exhibits \| The Tibetan Book of the Dead: Literature on Prayer, Ritual, and Meditation from the Religious Traditions of Tibet, India, and Nepal](https://explore.lib.virginia.edu/exhibits/show/dead/texts/transitions1?utm_source=chatgpt.com)\]
